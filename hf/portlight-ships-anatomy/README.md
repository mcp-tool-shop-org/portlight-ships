# portlight-ships anatomy dataset

**336 image/caption pairs** teaching ship anatomy — 14 hull types × 8 headings × 3
elevations. Every image is **pristine**; there is not one damaged example, by design.

Trigger token: **`plsvessel`**

## How it was made, and why that matters

These are **not** per-heading generations. Diffusion models cannot generate ship headings —
a ship is a near-symmetric object with no directional cue, so "port bow" and "starboard
quarter" both come back as the same broadside. Three prior attempts failed exactly that way.

So each hull is generated **once** as a single plate, converted to a **textured 3D mesh**
(Tripo v3.1), and rendered on a deterministic Blender turntable at 8 headings × 3 elevations
(15° / 30° / 50°). Identity holds across every heading because it is literally the same mesh.

Renders are 768×768 with true alpha, uniform lighting, and every hull normalised to the same
fore-aft length so the frame carries no scale information.

## Provenance

Every pair in this dataset follows a deterministic five-stage chain:

1. **Source plates and views — Nano Banana 2 (`Gemini 3.1 Flash Image`):** For each hull, a single pristine three-quarter front master plate (plus background-removed front, port profile, and stern views) was generated by **Nano Banana 2 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image)** via the ComfyUI partner-API node `GeminiNanoBanana2` (`pipeline/build_mesh_workflow.py`, `pipeline/build_pristine_workflow.py`), billed through the studio's paid Comfy Cloud plan.
2. **3D mesh — Tripo v3.1 (`v3.1-20260211`):** The three views (`[front no-bg, port profile, stern]`) were converted into a textured PBR `.glb` mesh by **Tripo v3.1** (`model_version: "v3.1-20260211"`, `texture=True`, `pbr=True`, `model_seed=42`, `texture_seed=42`, `geometry_quality="detailed"`, `texture_quality="detailed"`) via the ComfyUI partner-API node `TripoImageToModelNode` (`pipeline/build_mesh_workflow.py`), paid with Comfy Cloud credits.
3. **Normalisation — `pipeline/normalize_meshes.py` (trimesh):** Each raw Tripo `.glb` was uniformly rescaled so its fore-aft length equals `10.0` world units and translated to centre X/Z with the keel (`min Y`) at `Y = 0`.
4. **Turntable render — Blender 5.2 (`BLENDER_EEVEE_NEXT`):** Each normalised mesh was rendered by `pipeline/stage3_render_pack.py` (invoked by `pipeline/build_training_set.py`) using an orthographic camera (`--frame 12.9`, `--light 0.35`, `--world 0.18`) across **8 headings × 3 elevations** (`15°`, `30°`, `50°`) at **768×768 px** with true RGBA film transparency.
5. **Derived caption — `pipeline/build_training_set.py`:** Each `.txt` caption (and `metadata.jsonl` row) was assembled deterministically from the hull's structured YAML `rig_plan` and `signature_features` in the canon `.md` file, paired with the camera heading and elevation phrase.

### Wikipedia sourcing in the canon `.md` files

The anatomical specifications in the 14 hull canon files trace to historical vessel articles on English Wikipedia (`galleon.md` cites only internal studio generation and brand palette sources; the other 13 hull files cite Wikipedia URLs in their YAML `sources:` block). Inspecting all 14 canon `.md` files shows:

- **9 hull files cite and paraphrase Wikipedia without verbatim quotes** (`brig.md`, `brigantine.md`, `caravel.md`, `carrack.md`, `fluyt.md`, `frigate.md`, `schooner.md`, `sloop.md`, `xebec.md`).
- **4 hull files include short verbatim quoted phrases from Wikipedia inside their YAML `sources:` field** (`barque.md`, `cog.md`, `galley.md`, `junk.md`):
  - `barque.md` (from [Wikipedia: Barque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barque)) — quotes `'three or more masts of which the fore mast, mainmast, and any additional masts are rigged square, and only the aftmost mast (mizzen in three-masted barques) is rigged fore and aft'`, `'fore mast square-rigged'`, `'reserve fore-and-aft rigging for the aftmost mast for efficiency'`, `'almost half...apprentices'`, and `'the golden age of sail in the mid-19th century'`.
  - `cog.md` (from [Wikipedia: Cog (ship)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cog_(ship))) — quotes `'fitted with a single mast and a single square sail'`, `'large, rectangular, square-rigged'`, `'clinker-built cogs were effectively limited to a single sail'`, `'full lapstrake, or clinker, planking covering their sides'`, `'flush-laid flat bottom at midships which gradually shifted to overlapped strakes near the posts'`, `'double-clenched iron nails for plank fastenings'`, `'tarred moss...covered with wooden laths, and secured by metal staples'`, `'Both stem and stern-posts were straight and rather long, and connected to the keel-plank through intermediate pieces.'`, `'high castles at both bow and stern'`, `'stern-mounted, hanging, central rudder on a heavy stern-post'`, and `'a uniquely northern development'`.
  - `galley.md` (from [Wikipedia: Galley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley)) — quotes `'a heavy projection at the foot of the bow, sheathed with metal, usually bronze'`, `'basic square sails until the Early Middle Ages and later lateen sails'`, `'up to three rowers sharing a single bench, handling one oar each'`, `'rowers sharing a bench but using just a single large oar, sometimes with up to seven or more rowers per oar'`, and `'up to 46 m'`.
  - `junk.md` (from [Wikipedia: Junk (ship)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_(ship))) — quotes `'twelve down to three sails'`, `'full-length battens of the junk sail keep the sail flatter than ideal in all wind conditions'`, `'never lowered, but turned according to the direction of the wind'`, `'horseshoe-shaped stern supporting a high poop deck'`, `'watertight bulkheads'`, and `'up to twenty members of the crew to control in strong weather'`.

All prose bodies, `silhouette_cue` descriptions, `signature_features`, `forbidden_inputs`, and derived `.txt` training captions are original studio text. ## Captions are derived, never written

Each hull has a canon entry with a `rig_plan` naming every mast and sail. The caption's rig
phrase is **generated from that structure**:

```
plsvessel, a xebec, 3-masted sailing ship, lateen-rigged foremast, mainmast and mizzenmast,
4 sails set, three enormous triangular lateen sails on long angled yards, pronounced
overhanging bow and stern projecting past the waterline, pristine undamaged condition,
port broadside view, seen from a slightly elevated angle, game asset render on a plain background
```

Hand-written captions drift, and caption drift is how a LoRA learns fuzzy concepts. Deriving
them means "lateen" appears in every xebec caption, identically, every time.

One classifier bug found this way: an earlier version classed any mast carrying any square
sail as square-rigged, which made the brigantine's caption **byte-identical** to the brig's —
teaching the model the two vessels are the same thing. A mast is square-rigged only if it
carries a square *course*.

## Files

`<hull>__<elevation>__<heading>.png` + matching `.txt`, paired for the Hugging Face Dataset Viewer via `metadata.jsonl` (`file_name`, `text`, `hull`, `elevation`, `heading`):

- **hulls** — carrack, fluyt, cog, galleon, frigate, brig, barque, brigantine, schooner,
  sloop, caravel, xebec, galley, junk
- **elevations** — `low` (15°), `mid` (30°), `high` (50°)
- **headings** — front, front_left, left, back_left, back, back_right, right, front_right

Canon `.md` entries for all 14 hulls (plus `damage-tiers.md` and `traditions.md`) are included — each carries the rig plan, palette,
signature features, and the Wikipedia sourcing the anatomy traces to.

## Verification

Every image was checked before release:

- **zero clipping** — a clipped ship is a caption that lies about what is in the image.
  The frame requirement is fleet-dependent (adding the junk moved it from 12.04 to 12.78
  world units) so the build fails loudly rather than trusting a constant.
- **captions checked against renders** by opening them — the galley's oar banks appear on
  both sides as claimed, the cog's clinker planking survived as geometry, the caravel's
  cross device survived canon → plate → mesh → render.

## Known limitations

- **The brig and brigantine are too similar.** Their captions differ by a single clause and
  they have 24 images each; a model trained on this data learns one "two-masted square-ish
  ship" concept covering both. Confirmed by a 180-image fixed-seed sweep. If you use this
  data, expect that pair to be unreliable — the other 12 hulls separate cleanly.
- **The junk's battens are under-represented.** They are visible in the renders but a LoRA
  trained on 24 of them does not reproduce them.
- **Colours run ~13 points cooler** than the source palettes — the image-to-3D step
  desaturates when baking texture. Uniform across hulls.
